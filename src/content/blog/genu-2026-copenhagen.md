I had the opportunity to attend **GenU 2026 — Generative Models and Uncertainty Quantification**, a two-day workshop in Copenhagen bringing together researchers working across probabilistic modelling, generative AI, Bayesian inference, and uncertainty quantification.

The programme covered a broad range of perspectives, from diffusion models and adaptive learning to AI for scientific discovery. I particularly enjoyed Kevin Murphy’s talk on **LLM-assisted Bayesian experiment design for scientific discovery**, as well as Maike Osborne’s discussion on **AI governance and Bayesian thinking**.

I also presented a poster based on my [master’s thesis](/en/research/predig/), *On probabilistic prediction and information-theoretic operators*, where I study how predictive information can be used to identify informative and redundant observations in self-supervised learning. It was especially valuable to discuss the work with researchers thinking about closely related questions around predictive uncertainty and information, including Tom Rainforth.

Many thanks to **Pablo Moreno-Muñoz** for making it possible to continue developing this research, and to **the GenU organisers** for putting together such an interesting meeting in Copenhagen.
